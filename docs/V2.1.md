# V2.1 — the look, and getting data in by hand

> **Read this after [`V2.md`](V2.md).** V2 gave the home screen the right *structure* — a hero,
> four categorised panels, every metric in its pillar, logging in place. V2.1 is about how it
> **looks and reads**, and about one thing it could not do: get Apple Health data in without a
> server pipeline.

## 1. The owner's brief, in his words

> "currently it looks kinda bland… create one page which is a mirror of the today page but filled
> with sample data, how a completed day would look like. a completed day must look satisfying, the
> charts need to look good. currently also not all data is displayed on the first view, one needs
> to scroll down. it should utilize the full width in nice graphs in differing sizes, more
> important fields should be bigger. also add a little colour. its just all grey and white as of
> now. also the learned-something no longer asks what you learned."

And, of the health data:

> "there has to be a way to add the apple health data without the automation. lock in."

So V2.1 has four jobs:

1. **Make a finished day look like something you want to look at.** A page that shows the design
   at its best, with every metric filled.
2. **One screen.** At 1440 px wide the whole day must fit without scrolling. Wide graphs, tiles of
   different sizes, the important things bigger.
3. **Colour, meaningfully.** Not decoration: colour that says which part of life a number belongs
   to, and how it is going.
4. **A hand path for Apple Health.** No server, no automation, no account: a file you drop in.

---

## 2. Where the design language changes

`docs/spec/05-design.md` set the v1 language: *a quiet printed page*, one accent colour, no
gradients, no shadows, "the numbers are the ornament". V2.1 **keeps** the paper-and-ink base, the
hairlines, the tabular figures and the no-shadow rule — and **replaces** two of its rules for the
dashboard, deliberately and in writing:

| 05-design.md rule | V2.1 |
|---|---|
| `--accent` is the only saturated colour, at most twice per screen | four **pillar hues** (body, mind, attention, capital) plus the existing band tones. Colour is *semantic*: the hue says which part of life, the tone says how it is going. |
| (nothing about density) | the dashboard is a **tile grid** of differing sizes on the full width, not two equal columns. |

Everything else stands: tokens only, no shadow, no pure black/white, no icon pack, no chart
library, band **words** beside any band colour (`03-scoring.md` §4), and colour is never the only
carrier of meaning.

### 2.1 The palette (the tokens added in `src/styles/tokens.css`)

```
--card            the tile surface, a hair off the paper (never pure white)
                  light #FDFCFA · dark #1A1B1E
--card-sunken     chart plinths, control tracks
--warn-soft, --bad-soft, --good-soft   state backgrounds, used sparingly
--pillar-{body,mind,attention,capital}(-soft)
                  category identity: the band's name and tick, the tile's accent rule

                  light                        dark            hue
  body            #2F5D50  soft #DCE6E2        #7FB3A0  #1E2A27  pine
  mind            #3A4E8C  soft #E1E5F1        #9DAADB  #20253A  indigo
  attention       #266B75  soft #DCEDEF        #7FBDC7  #1B2C30  teal
  capital         #7C4A67  soft #F0E3EB        #C79BB8  #2A2128  plum
```

Tones are **not** new: `03-scoring.md` §4 already maps band → `--accent` / `--ink` / `--warn` /
`--bad`, and `scoreTone()` implements it. V2.1 uses those tones on charts, the ring and the score
figures, always next to the band word.

Contrast: every hue above is ≥ 4.5:1 on its own surface in both themes — the band name, the band
score and the tile's rule are all text-or-boundary in that hue, so it has to pass as one.

## 3. The one-screen tile grid

A single 12-column grid, `grid-auto-flow: dense`, gap `--s-3`. A tile's **span is its metric's
weight** — the weight is canonical (`02-metrics.md` §4), so "important" is not my opinion:

| weight | span | class | height | what it holds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ≥ 0.09 | 6 | `tile-lg` | `--tile-h-lg` | label, value, context line, a full chart |
| ≥ 0.05 | 4 | `tile-md` | `--tile-h-md` | label, value, a medium chart |
| ≥ 0.03 | 3 | `tile-md` | `--tile-h-md` | label, value, a small chart |
| < 0.03 | 2 | `tile-sm` | `--tile-h-sm` | label, value, or just the control |

Breakpoints: 12 columns ≥ 1120 px, 6 columns ≥ 720 px, 2 columns ≥ 480 px, 1 column below. At 320 px
a single column *must* scroll; that is the one place the one-screen rule cannot hold, and the
tiles stay 44 px-tappable instead.

Height budget: the target was the whole board on one 1440 × 900 screen. Measured at the end of the
build: **1173 px** tall on `/` (1250 on `/preview`, which adds the sample banner), with about
**1077 px of content above the footer** — a 122 px hero, four band headers and 19 tiles. So at a
900 px window it is one *short* scroll, not none. Everything that could be bought back honestly was
(hero 160 → 122, tile rows 128, the weakest-link line folded into the hero); the rest is bought with
either smaller charts or tap targets under 44 px, and the iron rules forbid both. Reported as a
deviation rather than hidden.

### 3.1 The tiles, by kind

- **Control tile** — the row you log by hand. The control *is* the readout (V2's rule, kept): the
  cells sit in the tile, with the value as a caption. Protein, creatine, greens, alcohol, read,
  clean day, the two monthly figures.
- **Chart tile** — a numeric metric: big figure, unit, the goal in words (`target 7.5 h`), and the
  chart. Sleep, sleep regularity, steps, distraction, gym, daylight, phone, social.
- **Score tile** — the day and the four pillars: a ring with the figure inside and the band word
  under it.

### 3.2 The charts

| chart | where | shape |
|---|---|---|
| `Ring` | the hero and every pillar header | 0–100 arc, tone-coloured, figure inside, band word under |
| `AreaChart` | the hero, 30 days | filled area, band rules at 60/75/90, latest dot, gaps left as gaps |
| `Bars` | 14 days (or 6 weeks) per metric | bars coloured by their own day's tone, a target hairline with its value, the latest bar solid, gaps left as gaps |
| `Sparkline` | tiles with no room for more | 30 points, tone-coloured |

All hand-written SVG on tokens. Every chart is `role="img"` with a sentence in `aria-label`, so it
is readable without seeing it.

## 4. `/preview` — a finished day

A mirror of `/` that renders the real components from **sample data**: 120 days, all 19 metrics,
100 % coverage, no rest days, no gaps, scoring around 88–92. It is the design's own test page and
the fastest way to judge a change.

- The data is pure and deterministic: `src/lib/sample.ts`, seeded, unit-tested.
- It is labelled **sample data** in the mode marker. It is interactive — the same tiles, the same
  taps — but the days live in the page's own memory for the length of the visit: nothing is
  written to IndexedDB, nothing is merged with your history, and a reload resets it to the seed.
- Reachable at `/preview` and with `G P`; linked from Settings. Not in the top nav (four links is
  the most that fits 320 px).

`/` and `/preview` share one view component so a change to the design lands on both, always.

## 5. "Learned something" asks again

`learned` is the only note-dependent score in the system (`03-scoring.md` §1.6): true with a
written line scores 100, true without one scores 40. `/log` has always revealed a one-line
`NoteField` under the row when the tick is on. The dashboard dropped that, so ticking it there
silently cost 60 points. V2.1 restores it: tick `learned` and the note field opens **inside the
tile** — never a modal, never a second screen.

## 6. Apple Health by hand — locked in

**Decision (this is the lock-in): the import format is `north.health.v1`, and the same file is what
a future iOS app would write.** Nothing else is allowed to be the contract.

```jsonc
{
  "format": "north.health.v1",
  "generated": "2026-10-18T21:20:00Z",   // when the file was produced
  "source": "apple-health-export",       // or "north-ios" later
  "days": [
    { "date": "2026-10-18",
      "sleepHours": 7.4, "sleepStart": "23:12",
      "steps": 9412, "strengthSessions": 1, "daylightMinutes": 34,
      "weightKg": 81.2 }
  ]
}
```

- Every field is optional; a day with none of them is invalid and is rejected. Values are the raw
  registry units, never derived scores.
- **A missing day stays a gap.** The importer never interpolates and never fills a day the file
  does not mention.
- **Only device metrics.** `sleepHours`, `sleepStart`, `steps`, `strengthSessions`,
  `daylightMinutes`, `weightKg` — exactly the registry's `autoField` sources. Nothing you log by
  hand is ever overwritten: a manually-written value wins (it carries `locked`).
- **Import is local.** The file is read in the browser, parsed, previewed and written to
  IndexedDB. No upload, no server, no account. The preview says exactly what will change before
  anything is written, and the result says what it kept.
- **What counts as a change.** A field the file brings that this device does not have, or has
  differently, is written with `src: 'import'` and no `locked` flag — so a later sync may refresh
  it. A field that already reads exactly this is reported as *already the same* and not written. A
  field a human typed is different, carries `locked`, and is reported back as *kept*.
- The converter for Apple's own export is a stdlib-only script (`server/health_export.py`):
  `export.xml` (900 MB, XML) → `north.health.v1`. It streams, it never keeps the file, and it
  prints only counts. It exists because Safari cannot parse a 900 MB XML in a tab.

Where it lives: **Settings → Your data → Apple Health**, beside the existing export/import.

## 7. What does not change

- The 19 metrics, their targets, weights and units (`02-metrics.md`), the scoring maths and bands
  (`03-scoring.md`), and the registry as the single source of truth.
- v1's list at `/log`. It is frozen and keeps working.
- Local-first, no account, no analytics, no runtime request beyond the self-hosted font.
- No new runtime dependency. Charts stay hand-written SVG.
- Keyboard-complete, 44 px targets, reduced motion honoured, AA contrast.
